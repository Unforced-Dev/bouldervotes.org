# Boulder elections: Civics 101

*For the November 3, 2026 election. This is a guide to who makes decisions, not a recommendation on how to vote.* [Source: https://bouldercounty.gov/elections/information/]

## Who runs the city?

Boulder has a **council–manager** government: the elected council sets city policy, and the city manager, hired by council, runs city administration and carries out that policy. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/boulder-city-council-frequently-asked-questions] The manager's office provides leadership and oversight to city departments; council is not the day-to-day supervisor of every employee. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/government/departments/city-managers-office] Council has nine members—the mayor and eight councilmembers—and each has an equal vote. [Source: https://library.municode.com/co/boulder/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=THCHBOCO_ARTIITHLEBOPODU_S8VA] All are elected **at large**, by voters across the whole city, not by neighborhood wards. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/boulder-city-council-frequently-asked-questions]

## What can the city decide?

**Housing and land use:** The city makes zoning and development decisions within city limits; the county has a separate land-use jurisdiction outside incorporated cities. [Sources: https://bouldercolorado.gov/government/departments/planning-development-services ; https://bouldercounty.gov/property-and-land/land-use/] State law also limits some local housing rules; a council candidate cannot alone rewrite state law. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/accessory-dwelling-units]

**Police and public safety:** Boulder has its own police department, while the Boulder County Sheriff's Office serves a separate county role. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/government/departments/police] Council sets city policy and funds services through the city budget; the manager administers city operations. [Sources: https://bouldercolorado.gov/boulder-city-council-frequently-asked-questions ; https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/budget]

**Money, parks and open space:** The city funds its own services and capital projects through its budget; its Open Space and Mountain Parks department manages city open-space lands. [Sources: https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/budget ; https://bouldercolorado.gov/government/departments/open-space-mountain-parks/about] Boulder County separately manages county open space. [Source: https://bouldercounty.gov/open-space/]

**Streets, transit and the airport:** City government is responsible for its municipal transportation facilities and Boulder Municipal Airport; regional transportation involves other governments and transit agencies too. [Sources: https://bouldercolorado.gov/government/departments/transportation-mobility ; https://bouldercolorado.gov/airport] The council voted in July 2026 to pursue federal airport funding; that was a council decision, not a November 2026 city ballot measure. [Sources: https://www.dailycamera.com/2026/07/27/boulder-municipal-airport-city-council-decision ; https://bouldercolorado.gov/2026-city-boulder-ballot-measures]

**Homelessness services:** The city, county and service organizations share this work; adults seeking countywide homeless services use Coordinated Entry. [Sources: https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/homelessness-services ; https://bouldercounty.gov/departments/community-services/homeless/homeless-services/]

**Schools:** City Council does **not** run Boulder Valley School District (BVSD). Its seven-member school board is elected separately by district voters, and BVSD's superintendent leads the district. [Sources: https://bvsd.org/about/board-of-education/meet-the-board ; https://bvsd.org/about] The Colorado state government sets statewide education rules and funding frameworks. [Source: https://ed.cde.state.co.us/cdefinance/generalinfo]

## How do we elect the mayor?

Before 2023, the council chose the mayor from among its members; voters approved direct election with ranked-choice voting in 2020, and **2023 was the first direct mayoral election**. [Sources: https://bouldercolorado.gov/guide/ranked-choice-voting-guide ; https://boulderreportinglab.org/2023/11/08/mayor-aaron-brockett-wins-close-race-to-become-boulders-first-directly-elected-city-mayor/] In the mayoral contest, rank one candidate first, another second if you wish, and so on; you do not have to rank everyone. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/guide/ranked-choice-voting-guide] If nobody has more than half the first-choice votes, the lowest candidate is eliminated and those ballots transfer to their next-ranked candidate still in the race; rounds continue until a winner is determined. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/guide/ranked-choice-voting-guide] Council seats are a separate election, not this mayoral ranked-choice contest. [Sources: https://bouldercolorado.gov/guide/ranked-choice-voting-guide ; https://bouldercolorado.gov/2026-city-boulder-mayoral-and-city-council-candidates]

## What's changing in 2026?

Voters will fill **five council seats and the mayor's office** on November 3, 2026. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/2026-city-boulder-mayoral-and-city-council-candidates] This is the first regular municipal candidate election after the move to even-numbered years. [Sources: https://bouldercolorado.gov/boulder-city-council-frequently-asked-questions ; https://boulderreportinglab.org/2026/08/30/meet-the-19-candidates-running-for-boulder-mayor-and-city-council-in-2026] The 2023 and 2025 council winners had temporary **three-year** terms for the transition; councilmembers elected in 2026 return to **four-year** terms. [Source: https://library.municode.com/co/boulder/codes/municipal_code?nodeId=THCHBOCO_ARTIITHLEBOPODU_S8VA] The mayor elected in 2023 had a three-year term; from 2026 onward, the mayoral term is **two years**. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/election-guidelines]

## How and when do I vote?

Boulder County mails general-election ballots and opens 24-hour drop boxes on **October 2, 2026**. [Source: https://bouldercounty.gov/elections/information/] Vote centers open **October 19**; find a drop box or vote center through the county elections site. [Sources: https://bouldercounty.gov/elections/information/ ; https://bouldercounty.gov/elections/information/ballot-drop-boxes] **There is no voter-registration deadline:** eligible voters can register and vote through Election Day, **November 3**; registering by **October 26** is important if you want a ballot mailed rather than getting one at a vote center. [Sources: https://bouldercounty.gov/elections/information/ ; https://bouldercounty.gov/elections/information/ballot-services/] Mail ballots must be **received by 7 p.m. November 3**, not merely postmarked; the county recommends mailing them no later than October 26, or using a drop box afterward. [Source: https://bouldercounty.gov/elections/information/]

## Who pays for campaigns?

City candidates and ballot-measure committees must disclose campaign money under Boulder election rules; the city posts committee filings and reports. [Sources: https://bouldercolorado.gov/election-guidelines ; https://bouldercolorado.gov/elections/election-committee-filings] A donor may give an official candidate committee no more than **$100** in total. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/election-guidelines] Qualifying candidates can choose public matching funds: for 2026, they must first raise at least **$2,473** in eligible individual contributions and accept a **$24,731** campaign spending limit; the city can match eligible fundraising up to **$12,366**. [Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/election-guidelines] Participating candidates agree to accept donations only from natural persons, not corporations or nonprofits; receiving public funds is optional, and the city's candidate list marks which candidates are participating. [Sources: https://bouldercolorado.gov/election-guidelines ; https://bouldercolorado.gov/2026-city-boulder-mayoral-and-city-council-candidates]
