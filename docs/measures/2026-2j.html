2026 ballot · All city measures

2J: Vacancy excise tax

This would tax homes occupied for 183 days or fewer in a year, starting at $4,000 per vacant home. The tax could rise with inflation to no more than $7,000; money would support general city services rather than a dedicated housing fund. The ballot's $6 million figure is an estimate for 2028, not a guaranteed collection amount.

A YES vote means: Create the residential vacancy tax and authorize the city to retain and spend its revenue.

A NO vote means: Do not create this proposed residential vacancy tax.

Money

Ballot language estimates $6 million in 2028, the first full collection year. Initial rate is $4,000 per qualifying vacant home, indexed to inflation but capped at $7,000; actual collections depend on how many homes qualify and whether owners change how they use them. The $6 million figure is a ballot revenue estimate, not a guaranteed collection amount. source

How it got on the ballot

Referred August 6, 2026, by a 7–1 council vote; Nicole Speer voted no. source

Full ballot text

Official text on the City of Boulder website

Read the full ballot wording here
Shall the City of Boulder taxes be increased $6,000,000 annually (which amount represents estimated revenues in 2028, the first full fiscal year of collection), and by such amounts raised annually thereafter, by imposing a $4,000 tax on vacant homes that are occupied for 183 days or less per year, with such amount never falling below $4,000 but increasing annually in accordance with the Denver-Aurora-Lakewood consumer price index up to, but never exceeding $7,000, with the revenue from such tax to be used for the purpose of supporting City services, including: police and fire protection; parks and recreation; transportation and maintenance; and other general services important to the quality of life in the City; and shall the revenues from such taxes and any related earnings be collected, retained, and spent as a voter-approved revenue change without limitation and an exception to the revenue and spending limits of Article X, Section 20 of the Colorado Constitution?
