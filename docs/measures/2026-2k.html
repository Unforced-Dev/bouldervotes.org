City of Boulder ballot measure 2K · 2026

2K: Building Boulder Together: Recreation and Safety Bond

A YES vote means
Authorize up to $400 million in general-obligation debt and a property-tax levy to repay it, within the ballot's stated annual tax and total repayment ceilings.
A NO vote means
Do not authorize this bond and its associated property-tax increase; existing building needs would still have to be addressed through other choices or existing funds.

In plain words

This would authorize up to $400 million in city borrowing, repaid with additional property taxes, for recreation and public-safety buildings and other capital projects. The ballot lists example projects, including both recreation centers, fire stations, a police/911 building, the Penfield Tate II building and the Municipal Service Center; exact allocations are not fixed in the ballot question. The maximum repayment with financing costs is $650 million. The city's project guide identifies intended projects and estimated costs, not a binding project-by-project appropriation.

Money

Debt cap $400 million, maximum total repayment $650 million, and annual tax increase up to $32.5 million. The city estimates that, if it borrowed the full $400 million, a $1 million residential property would pay about $400 more each year for roughly 20 years; borrowing less or in stages changes the tax impact. The listed project estimates total more than $400 million, so not every estimate is guaranteed to be fully covered by this bond. source

How it got on the ballot

Council unanimously referred the bond at its August 6 meeting, then scheduled final ballot wording with an explicit lap pool for August 20; the current city ballot guide carries that amended language. The precise final-reading roll call has not been independently confirmed here. source

Full ballot text

Official text on the City of Boulder website

Read the full ballot wording here
Shall City of Boulder debt be increased up to $400,000,000, with a maximum repayment cost up to $650,000,000, and shall city taxes be increased up to $32,500,000 annually for the purpose of financing the construction, renovation, or replacement of community recreation, safety infrastructure, and other capital projects, such as: (I) the South Boulder Recreation Center, including a lap pool; (II) the North Boulder Recreation Center, including aquatics amenities and improvements needed to co-locate West Age Well Senior Center; (III) fire stations (to sustain emergency and wildfire response capabilities); (IV) a new Public Safety Building for police and 911 services; (V) Penfield Tate II Municipal Building; and (VI) the Municipal Service Center (to support infrastructure services, such as snow removal, utility maintenance, and city street operations); In order to support recreation, safety, and critical infrastructure needs for community members of all ages; Through the issuance and payment of general obligation debt, with such debt containing such terms, not inconsistent herewith, as the City Council may determine; and in connection therewith, Shall ad valorem property taxes be levied without limitation as to the rate, but not more than the amounts listed above, to generate an amount sufficient in each year to pay the principal of, premium, if any, and interest on such debt or any refunding debt (or to create a reserve for such payment); and Shall the proceeds of such debt and reserves and the revenues from such taxes and any investment income earned from such proceeds and revenues be collected and spent without limitation or condition as a voter-approved revenue change and an exception to the limits that would otherwise apply under article x, section 20 of the Colorado constitution or any other law?
