Boulder's November 3, 2026 election

A plain guide to the City of Boulder ballot: who is running, what each measure does, and who is backing whom — every fact with its source. We do not endorse, score, or recommend.

Ballots are mailed starting Friday, October 2.

Your ballot must be received by 7 p.m. Tuesday, November 3.

Mail it early or use a 24-hour drop box (they open October 2). Dates from Boulder County Elections. October 2 is the planned mailing date, not a delivery guarantee.

What is on your ballot

  • Mayor — 6 candidates, one seat. You rank your choices (ranked-choice voting).
  • City Council — 13 candidates for five seats. Vote for up to five; the top five win.
  • City measures — 4 questions: 2J, 2K, 2L, 2M.

New to how the city works? Read Civics 101: who decides what, how ranked-choice works, and who pays for campaigns.

County, state, school-board and regional items are also on your ballot; this site covers the City of Boulder only.

City measures

2J: Vacancy excise tax

This would tax homes occupied for 183 days or fewer in a year, starting at $4,000 per vacant home. The tax could rise with inflation to no more than $7,000; money would support general city services rather than a dedicated housing fund. The ballot's $6 million figure is an estimate for 2028, not a guaranteed collection amount.

2K: Building Boulder Together: Recreation and Safety Bond

This would authorize up to $400 million in city borrowing, repaid with additional property taxes, for recreation and public-safety buildings and other capital projects. The ballot lists example projects, including both recreation centers, fire stations, a police/911 building, the Penfield Tate II building and the Municipal Service Center; exact allocations are not fixed in the ballot question. The maximum repayment with financing costs is $650 million. The city's project guide identifies intended projects and estimated costs, not a binding project-by-project appropriation.

2L: Collective bargaining for firefighters (new Charter §73)

This would put full-time firefighters' collective-bargaining rights into the city charter. Bargaining already exists under a city contract and ordinance; the measure would make the right a charter provision and establish a process for unresolved disputes, potentially ending in a city vote. The city retains certain management rights defined in the proposed amendment.

2M: Charter debt-limit calculation (actual vs. assessed value)

This would calculate the city's charter debt limit using actual property value instead of assessed value while retaining the three-percent rate. Because actual value is larger, it would increase the city's legal borrowing capacity. It is a companion question to the $400 million bond, but passing 2M alone would not issue that bond or directly impose the bond's property tax.
