What's on the ballot

4 city measures

Four city measures. Each page starts with what a Yes vote and a No vote mean, then the money, the full ballot wording, and who supports or opposes it.

Ballot measure 2J

2JVacancy excise tax

This would tax homes occupied for 183 days or fewer in a year, starting at $4,000 per vacant home.

What Yes and No mean on 2J

Ballot measure 2K

2KBuilding Boulder Together: Recreation and Safety Bond

This would authorize up to $400 million in city borrowing, repaid with additional property taxes, for recreation and public-safety buildings and other capital projects.

What Yes and No mean on 2K

Ballot measure 2L

2LCollective bargaining for firefighters (new Charter §73)

This would put full-time firefighters' collective-bargaining rights into the city charter.

What Yes and No mean on 2L

Ballot measure 2M

2MCharter debt-limit calculation (actual vs. assessed value)

This would calculate the city's charter debt limit using actual property value instead of assessed value while retaining the three-percent rate.

What Yes and No mean on 2M

How to use this guide

  1. Start with a race. Open a candidate above. The top of each page says the office, a one-line bio, and links to their positions, forum answers, endorsements and money.
  2. Read their own words. Quotes come with the source under them. Long answers fold; tap “Read the full answer”.
  3. Check where an endorsement comes from. Each one is labelled: the endorser's own statement, a city filing, a news listing, or “X campaign lists Y” when only the campaign says so.
  4. Read a measure. Each measure page leads with what Yes and No mean.
  5. Print it. Every candidate has a printable sheet. New to city government? Read Civics 101.
