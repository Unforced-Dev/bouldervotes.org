## Election basics

- Boulder County mails ballots to registered voters starting October 2, 2026. 24-hour drop boxes open the same day.
- Ballots must be received (not postmarked) by 7 p.m. on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. The county recommends mailing by October 26; after that, use a drop box or Vote Center.
- Vote Centers open October 19 for in-person voting and same-day registration.
- You can register and vote through Election Day. Register by October 26 if you want a ballot mailed to you.
- Only registered voters who live inside Boulder city limits get these city races. Check your registration at https://www.govotecolorado.gov
- Key dates: 2026-10-02: Ballots mailed; 24-hour drop boxes open; 2026-10-19: Vote Centers open for in-person voting; 2026-10-26: Last recommended day to return a ballot by mail; 2026-11-03: Election Day: ballots must be received by 7 p.m.. Source: https://bouldercounty.gov/elections/information/
- Mayor: Rank the candidates: a 1st choice, then a 2nd, 3rd and so on if you want. You don't have to rank everyone. Term: two years.
- City Council: Vote for up to five. The five candidates with the most votes win. Term: four years.
- Only City of Boulder races and measures. Ballots also carry county, state, school board and regional items, which this guide does not cover.

## City ballot measures

### 2J: Vacancy excise tax

Page: https://bouldervotes.org/measures/2026-2j.html
A YES vote means: Create the residential vacancy tax and authorize the city to retain and spend its revenue.
A NO vote means: Do not create this proposed residential vacancy tax.
In plain words: This would tax homes occupied for 183 days or fewer in a year, starting at $4,000 per vacant home. The tax could rise with inflation to no more than $7,000; money would support general city services rather than a dedicated housing fund. The ballot's $6 million figure is an estimate for 2028, not a guaranteed collection amount.
Money: Ballot language estimates $6 million in 2028, the first full collection year. Initial rate is $4,000 per qualifying vacant home, indexed to inflation but capped at $7,000; actual collections depend on how many homes qualify and whether owners change how they use them. The $6 million figure is a ballot revenue estimate, not a guaranteed collection amount. Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/2026-city-boulder-ballot-measures
How it got on the ballot: Referred August 6, 2026, by a 7–1 council vote; Nicole Speer voted no. Source: https://boulderreportinglab.org/2026/08/06/boulder-city-council-sends-vacancy-tax-and-400-million-bond-to-november-ballot-rejects-downtown-development-authority/

### 2K: Building Boulder Together: Recreation and Safety Bond

Page: https://bouldervotes.org/measures/2026-2k.html
A YES vote means: Authorize up to $400 million in general-obligation debt and a property-tax levy to repay it, within the ballot's stated annual tax and total repayment ceilings.
A NO vote means: Do not authorize this bond and its associated property-tax increase; existing building needs would still have to be addressed through other choices or existing funds.
In plain words: This would authorize up to $400 million in city borrowing, repaid with additional property taxes, for recreation and public-safety buildings and other capital projects. The ballot lists example projects, including both recreation centers, fire stations, a police/911 building, the Penfield Tate II building and the Municipal Service Center; exact allocations are not fixed in the ballot question. The maximum repayment with financing costs is $650 million. The city's project guide identifies intended projects and estimated costs, not a binding project-by-project appropriation.
Money: Debt cap $400 million, maximum total repayment $650 million, and annual tax increase up to $32.5 million. The city estimates that, if it borrowed the full $400 million, a $1 million residential property would pay about $400 more each year for roughly 20 years; borrowing less or in stages changes the tax impact. The listed project estimates total more than $400 million, so not every estimate is guaranteed to be fully covered by this bond. Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/guide/recreation-and-safety-bond
How it got on the ballot: Council unanimously referred the bond at its August 6 meeting, then scheduled final ballot wording with an explicit lap pool for August 20; the current city ballot guide carries that amended language. The precise final-reading roll call has not been independently confirmed here. ### 2L: Collective bargaining for firefighters (new Charter §73)

Page: https://bouldervotes.org/measures/2026-2l.html
A YES vote means: Add the specified collective-bargaining right and dispute process to the charter.
A NO vote means: Do not add this charter protection; existing contract and ordinance arrangements are not themselves repealed by a no vote.
In plain words: This would put full-time firefighters' collective-bargaining rights into the city charter. Bargaining already exists under a city contract and ordinance; the measure would make the right a charter provision and establish a process for unresolved disputes, potentially ending in a city vote. The city retains certain management rights defined in the proposed amendment.
Money: The published ballot question has no specific tax, debt, or dollar amount. Future negotiated labor costs are not quantified in the ballot language; an unresolved impasse could lead to an election under the proposed process. ### 2M: Charter debt-limit calculation (actual vs. assessed value)

Page: https://bouldervotes.org/measures/2026-2m.html
A YES vote means: Amend the charter's debt-limit calculation to three percent of actual taxable property value, increasing capacity to borrow.
A NO vote means: Keep the current debt-limit calculation based on assessed property value; the proposed bond may not be issuable within the existing limit.
In plain words: This would calculate the city's charter debt limit using actual property value instead of assessed value while retaining the three-percent rate. Because actual value is larger, it would increase the city's legal borrowing capacity. It is a companion question to the $400 million bond, but passing 2M alone would not issue that bond or directly impose the bond's property tax.
Money: No direct tax increase or new bond is authorized by this question alone. The city's legal debt ceiling would increase by switching the base from assessed to actual value; the resulting dollar limit depends on taxable property value. A contemporaneous advocacy presentation estimated a change from about $152.7 million to $1.55 billion; treat these as estimates, not official ballot language. Source: https://bouldercolorado.gov/2026-city-boulder-ballot-measures
